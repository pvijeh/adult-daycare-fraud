# The New York City Social Adult Day Care Industry

Last reviewed: August 2026

This primer explains how New York City Social Adult Day Care (SADC) programs
fit into the long-term-care system, which organizations oversee them, how money
and records move through the industry, and which patterns can responsibly be
investigated for possible fraud.

It is a reporting guide, not legal advice. Requirements change, and any
provider-level claim must be tied to the rule and effective date that governed
the period being described.

## 1. What a Social Adult Day Care program is

NYC Aging describes an SADC as a structured program for functionally impaired
adults that provides socialization, supervision and monitoring, personal care,
and nutrition in a protective setting. The New York State Office for the Aging
describes Social Adult Day Services (SADS) as long-term-care services for
people with physical or cognitive impairments, delivered under a
person-centered service plan.

Common objectives include:

- Reducing isolation.
- Supporting daily functioning and safety.
- Providing meals, activities, supervision, and some personal care.
- Giving family and other informal caregivers respite.
- Helping participants remain in their homes and communities.

The population is not limited to people over 65. Eligibility and service need
are based on functional, cognitive, and psychosocial impairment, program rules,
and the payer's assessment.

Official background:

- [NYC Aging SADC page](https://www.nyc.gov/site/dfta/community-partners/social-adult-day-care.page)
- [NYS Office for the Aging SADS page](https://aging.ny.gov/social-adult-day-services-sads)
- [Title 9 NYCRR §6654.20 materials](https://aging.ny.gov/15-pi-12-social-adult-day-services-monitoring-requirements-0)

## 2. SADC is not Adult Day Health Care

The similar names conceal an important distinction.

| Social Adult Day Care / Social Adult Day Services | Adult Day Health Care |
| --- | --- |
| Social and long-term-support model | Medical model |
| Socialization, supervision, nutrition, personal care, activities, and caregiver respite | Nursing, therapies, medical social services, rehabilitation, clinical assessment, and other health services |
| NYC sites register with NYC Aging | Programs operate under NYS Department of Health requirements, often through nursing homes or diagnostic and treatment centers |
| Registration is not a license or quality certification | Programs are approved and subject to health-care program standards |
| Common Medicaid code in public spending data: `S5105` | Common Medicaid code in public spending data: `S5102` |

NYS Department of Health's
[Adult Day Health Care page](https://www.health.ny.gov/health_care/medicaid/program/longterm/addc.htm)
states that ADHC participants require nursing-home-level care and a physician's
order and that the program can include nursing, physical and occupational
therapy, speech pathology, and other clinical services.

An investigation must not combine SADC and ADHC providers merely because both
use "adult day care" in a name or taxonomy search.

## 3. The operating chain

The industry is best understood as a chain of distinct parties and records:

```text
Medicaid / Medicare
        |
        v
NYS Department of Health
        |
        v
Managed Long Term Care plan
        |
        +---- contracts with and oversees ----> SADC legal entity
                                                |
                                                v
                                          SADC facility/site
                                                |
                                                v
                                           participants
```

Other entities sit alongside that chain:

- NYC Aging registers NYC SADC sites, receives complaints, and can issue
  notices of violation under city law.
- NYS Office for the Aging publishes program standards and monitoring tools.
- OMIG receives site-level SADC certifications and can exclude Medicaid
  providers.
- HHS-OIG can impose federal exclusions.
- NYC Health inspects food-service operations.
- NYC Department of Buildings controls building and occupancy records.
- OATH adjudicates many city summonses.
- The Department of State records corporate entities.
- A property owner may be unrelated to the SADC operator.

Each party creates a different type of record. A registry row, NPI, contract,
claim, inspection, summons, exclusion, and court judgment are not
interchangeable.

## 4. Registration, certification, licensure, and participation

These terms must remain separate.

### NYC Aging registration

All SADCs operating in New York City must register with NYC Aging. The city's
[official page](https://www.nyc.gov/site/dfta/community-partners/social-adult-day-care.page)
expressly states that registration does **not** grant a certification or
license and does not represent a determination about service quality.

Registration establishes that a facility supplied information to the city's
registry. It does not establish:

- Medicaid enrollment.
- An active MLTC contract.
- A valid NPI for the site.
- An approved certificate of occupancy.
- That services were delivered.
- That claims were accurate.

### Program standards

The NYS Office for the Aging identifies
[Title 9 NYCRR §6654.20](https://aging.ny.gov/social-adult-day-services-sads)
as the principal SADS program standard. Funding and payer relationships affect
which agency monitors compliance, while NYC law separately requires NYC SADCs
to register and comply with applicable laws and rules.

### OMIG certification

OMIG's
[SADC certification form](https://apps.omig.ny.gov/sadc/sadccertification.aspx)
states that a separate form must be submitted for each location that contracts
with an MLTC plan. The form asks the provider to certify information including
site and compliance representations.

The form is public, but a complete public history of provider submissions,
amendments, and agency validation was not identified.

### MLTC contracting and oversight

Managed Long Term Care plans organize long-term services for people with
chronic illness or disabilities who wish to remain in the community. NYS
Department of Health explains the program in its
[MLTC overview](https://www.health.ny.gov/health_care/managed_care/mltc/mltc_overview.htm).

The Department's
[SADC compliance portal](https://www.health.ny.gov/health_care/managed_care/mltc/sadc/)
and
[contracting and oversight guidance](https://www.health.ny.gov/health_care/managed_care/mltc/sadc/contracting-oversight_roles-responsibilities.htm)
describe plan responsibilities that include initial and annual in-person
evaluations, remediation, records, and oversight of contracted sites.

The existence of a facility does not prove that every MLTC plan has contracted
with it, and a plan-directory appearance does not establish the exact contract
or payment period.

## 5. The site-level NPI transition

An NPI identifies a health-care provider record; it does not license a
facility. Historically, public network data showed some SADCs using one NPI
across multiple addresses.

[MLTC Policy 25.05](https://www.health.ny.gov/health_care/medicaid/redesign/mrt90/mltc_policy/2025/25-05.htm)
requires plans, by December 8, 2025, to verify that SADC locations have an
organization NPI with:

- Adult Day Care taxonomy `261QA0600X`.
- The physical SADC location as the primary practice address.
- A separate NPI for each location.
- Reporting in the relevant PNDS quarter.
- Use on claims and encounters for members receiving SADC services.

This policy makes site-level NPI reconciliation substantially more useful after
its effective date. It does **not** make earlier multi-location NPI use
retroactively improper.

## 6. How SADC services are paid

Many SADC participants receive services through Medicaid Managed Long Term
Care. The simplified payment relationship is:

1. Medicaid pays or funds an MLTC plan under the plan's approved payment
   arrangement.
2. The plan assesses members, authorizes covered services, contracts with
   providers, and submits network and encounter information.
3. The SADC bills or reports services through the arrangement established with
   the plan.
4. Billing and servicing NPIs, the site, and the legal entity may differ.

Public HHS data includes aggregated Medicaid activity for HCPCS `S5105`, but a
provider-month row is not a claim image, attendance record, or proof that one
specific facility received the full amount.

Important distinctions:

- **Billing NPI:** The provider identified as billing.
- **Servicing NPI:** The provider identified as furnishing the service.
- **Facility/site:** The physical location where the participant was expected
  to receive the service.
- **Legal entity:** The corporation or organization operating or contracting
  for the site.
- **Sponsor name:** The entity name stated in the NYC Aging registry.
- **MLTC plan:** The payer/oversight organization reporting a network and
  encounters.

Any spending analysis that collapses these roles can create false
provider-level totals.

## 7. The provider identity problem

A single real-world operation can create many identifiers:

| Identifier | What it identifies | Common failure mode |
| --- | --- | --- |
| DFTA ID | NYC Aging registration record/site | Missing history or stale site information |
| NPI | Federal provider identity record | One NPI reused across locations; stale practice address |
| MMIS ID | NY Medicaid enrollment record | Current snapshot mistaken for full history |
| DOS ID | New York legal entity | DBA name differs from legal name |
| BBL | Tax lot | Multiple buildings, addresses, or tenants on one lot |
| BIN | Building | Multiple floors, suites, and unrelated tenants |
| PNDS observation | Plan/provider/location/quarter | Reporting error mistaken for a contract fact |
| OATH respondent | Party named on a summons | Property owner or other tenant mistaken for the SADC |

The defensible unit for physical analysis is generally:

```text
premises = BBL + building + floor/suite + effective period
```

Normalizing away floor and suite is useful for some address matching, but
unsafe for co-location or capacity conclusions. Two facilities on different
floors of one building are not the same premises.

## 8. Industry structure that can look suspicious but be legitimate

The following patterns deserve review but have ordinary explanations:

- **One sponsor with multiple sites:** A legitimate chain or operator.
- **Shared phone:** Central intake, a management office, or an answering
  service.
- **Shared authorized official:** Common ownership or an executive overseeing
  multiple facilities.
- **Shared NPI before the site-level policy:** Historical practice that may
  have been allowed or tolerated at the time.
- **NPPES address mismatch:** A stale federal record, mailing address, recent
  move, or normalization error.
- **Multiple SADCs in one building:** Separate floors/suites, co-working
  administrative space, or a multi-tenant commercial property.
- **Residential PLUTO building class:** Mixed-use lot classification rather
  than the legal use of the facility's space.
- **No obvious food permit:** Catered meals, a permit under another entity, or
  incomplete matching.
- **High Medicaid activity:** A large legitimate participant population, third-
  party billing, or one NPI representing multiple sites.
- **Disappearance from an annual list:** Closure, relocation, rebrand, delayed
  reporting, or a PDF extraction failure.

These are reasons to retrieve better records, not reasons to label a business
fraudulent.

## 9. Fraud and abuse typologies worth investigating

Official SADC cases have described conduct such as kickbacks, participant
recruitment, billing for services not provided, and laundering proceeds through
related entities. For example:

- A
  [2025 DOJ guilty plea](https://www.justice.gov/opa/pr/leader-68m-adult-day-care-fraud-scheme-pleads-guilty)
  described kickbacks and Medicaid billing for services that were not provided
  or were induced by kickbacks.
- A
  [2026 DOJ complaint](https://www.justice.gov/opa/pr/two-queens-men-charged-120m-adult-day-care-and-pharmacy-fraud-medicare-and-medicaid)
  alleged adult-day-care and pharmacy claims, kickbacks, and services not
  provided. A complaint is an allegation, not a conviction.

Those cases support investigative typologies, not inferences about unrelated
providers.

### Services not provided

**Theory:** Medicaid activity was reported for days when a participant did not
attend or no covered service occurred.

**Potential public leads:**

- Activity after a verified closure, exclusion, or contract termination.
- Volume inconsistent with a verified site's capacity and operating schedule.
- Billing and servicing NPI tied to a different site during the same period.

**Evidence needed:**

- Claims/encounters.
- Attendance and transportation logs.
- Participant interviews under appropriate safeguards.
- Staffing and service documentation.
- Exact site capacity and operating dates.

### Kickbacks or improper inducements

**Theory:** Cash, gifts, or other benefits were offered to induce enrollment or
attendance tied to federally funded services.

**Potential public leads:** Public enforcement records, recruiter relationships,
related cash-intensive entities, or testimony in court records.

**Evidence needed:** Financial records, communications, witness testimony,
undercover evidence, or an official investigation. Open registry and property
data cannot detect a kickback.

### Billing outside an authorized relationship

**Theory:** Activity occurred before enrollment, after exclusion, outside an
MLTC contract, after termination, or under an NPI not valid for the site.

**Potential public leads:**

- Dated HHS activity.
- Medicaid enrollment dates.
- PNDS network observations.
- Exclusion effective/reinstatement dates.
- Site-level NPI policy dates.

**Evidence needed:** Contract and termination records, claims/encounters,
enrollment history, and the rule effective during the activity.

### Identity substitution or cross-site billing

**Theory:** One site, legal entity, or NPI was used to bill for services
associated with another.

**Potential public leads:**

- One NPI across multiple physical locations after site-level requirements.
- Billing and servicing NPI relationships that do not reconcile to the site.
- Practice address, plan network address, and registry address disagreement.

**Evidence needed:** Site-specific NPI history, claims/encounters, contracts,
certifications, and operating records.

### Impossible or implausible capacity

**Theory:** Reported utilization exceeds what the premises and schedule could
support.

**Potential public leads:** Verified capacity, facility-level square footage,
operating hours, participant-month activity, and transportation patterns.

**Evidence needed:** Registered capacity, the actual leased premises area,
certificate-of-occupancy schedule, daily attendance, session duration, and
claims. PLUTO lot area alone is not enough.

### Falsified certification or compliance records

**Theory:** A provider certified facts about occupancy, staffing, site
identity, or compliance that contradicted records known at the time.

**Potential public leads:** Certification submission compared with dated CoO,
NPI, registry, and inspection records.

**Evidence needed:** The signed certification and amendments, source documents
for the same effective date, and evidence about knowledge or intent.

### Related-entity money movement

**Theory:** Payments moved among providers, recruiters, pharmacies, home-care
entities, landlords, or shell companies to conceal proceeds or fund
inducements.

**Potential public leads:** Shared officers, addresses, property transactions,
corporate filings, nonprofit disclosures, and relationships described in court
records.

**Evidence needed:** Bank records, contracts, invoices, beneficial ownership,
communications, and official investigative findings. A network graph is not
proof of conspiracy.

## 10. Oversight gaps that shape the industry

The current public record is fragmented:

1. NYC Aging registration is not a license or quality certification.
2. NPPES identifies an NPI but does not establish Medicaid enrollment or
   operation.
3. Medicaid enrollment is separate from MLTC contracting and quarterly network
   reporting.
4. Public spending is aggregated by NPI and month, not by verified facility and
   participant.
5. Facility capacity and historical status are not available in a single
   machine-readable public source.
6. Complaint records and remediation histories are mostly unavailable at the
   facility level.
7. Certificate-of-occupancy metadata often omits the floor-level details needed
   for a capacity or use conclusion.
8. Corporate open data emphasizes active entities and misses dissolved
   companies.

The strongest public-interest story may therefore be about fragmented
oversight and weak public transparency, even when available records are
insufficient to prove widespread fraud.

## 11. Evidence-safe investigative workflow

### Step 1: Establish the facility

- Start with the NYC Aging DFTA ID.
- Preserve the exact program name, sponsor, address, floor/suite, phone, BIN,
  BBL, and retrieval date.
- Build a year-by-year registry timeline.

### Step 2: Resolve identities

- Match NPI, MMIS ID, DOS entity, and plan/network observations.
- Separate legal entity, DBA/program name, corporate mailing address, site,
  property owner, billing NPI, and servicing NPI.
- Queue ambiguous matches for human review.

### Step 3: Establish effective dates

- Registration/list date.
- Enrollment period.
- MLTC network/contract period.
- NPI update and site-level policy date.
- Exclusion and reinstatement date.
- Inspection, summons, finding, and disposition dates.
- Claims/activity month.

### Step 4: Generate observations, not a fraud score

Examples:

- `data discrepancy` — NYC Aging and NPPES addresses differ.
- `requires records` — capacity is unavailable.
- `lead` — exact phone is shared by multiple registered sites.
- `compliance concern` — a verified rule and dated record conflict.
- `allegation` — an unresolved complaint or charge.
- `enforcement record` — an exact, reviewed exclusion, sustained finding,
  plea, judgment, or other official action.

### Step 5: Seek disconfirming evidence

For every observation, record:

- Plausible benign explanations.
- Sources that would resolve the issue.
- Evidence that contradicts the initial theory.
- Match confidence and known coverage gaps.

### Step 6: Apply the publication gate

Provider-level publication should require:

- At least two strong identity identifiers where available.
- Date overlap with the operating period.
- The governing rule and effective date for compliance claims.
- Accurate legal disposition language.
- Source URLs and record identifiers.
- Human review, especially for natural persons, allegations, and enforcement
  matches.

## 12. What this project should and should not claim

### Defensible claims

- "NYC Aging listed 359 facilities in the retrieved registry."
- "The retrieved NPPES record for NPI X used a different address."
- "PNDS datasets reported NPI X at these locations in these quarters."
- "HHS published aggregate S5105 activity associated with NPI X through month
  Y."
- "OMIG's exclusion list named entity X effective on date Y."
- "DOB metadata did not expose the occupancy schedule needed to evaluate
  capacity."

### Claims that require stronger evidence

- "The facility billed for people who did not attend."
- "The provider operated after termination."
- "The site exceeded legal capacity."
- "The owner used shell companies to conceal proceeds."
- "A shared phone, address, officer, or NPI proves common control or fraud."
- "High spending, rapid growth, neighborhood saturation, or limited web
  presence is suspicious."

## 13. Recommended product boundary

The public application should provide:

- A neutral provider directory.
- Facility registration and identity timelines.
- Independently verifiable observations grouped by evidence label.
- Source, date, confidence, limitation, benign explanation, and records-needed
  fields.
- Area-level density clearly separated from provider conduct.
- No aggregate fraud score, percentile, risk tier, or anomaly ranking.

If an internal triage system is ever built, it should be a separate,
access-controlled artifact used to allocate reporting time. It should not be
deployed through the public Vercel application or exposed in public API
payloads.
